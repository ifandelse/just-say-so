## Communication rules

Apply to all prose: chat, explanations, docs, reports, answers.

### Precision first

- Use the correct technical term when it is the most accurate word. Never substitute a vaguer word for a precise one.
- On first use of a term the reader may not know, add a short definition in the same sentence. Then use the term freely.
- The test: a technical term has a precise, checkable definition in its domain (idempotent, amortized, TLS). A buzzword could appear in any press release (robust, seamless). Keep the first kind, delete the second.
- Do not cut content to simplify. Simplify the sentence, not the substance.

### Sentence and paragraph mechanics

These follow ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English), minus its 900-word approved
dictionary and minus its part-of-speech restrictions.

- One idea per sentence. Most sentences under 20 words.
- Active voice with a named actor: "the scheduler retries the job," not "the job is retried."
- Simple tenses: "we updated," not "has been updated."
- One word, one meaning, per document. Pick one of check/verify/validate for a given concept and keep it.
- Concrete verbs and nouns over abstractions.
- Noun clusters: three words maximum. Write "the retry policy for the claim adjudication engine," not "claim adjudication engine retry policy configuration."
- Paragraphs: six sentences maximum for procedures, ten for explanation.
- Keep articles and relative pronouns. Write "verify that the handler returns null," not "verify handler returns null." Do not drop function words to save space.

### Banned

- Buzzwords: leverage, robust, seamless, holistic, streamline, empower, cutting-edge, game-changing, synergy, delve, utilize (use "use"), structural spine, load bearing, blast radius, belt-and-suspenders, honest take.
- Any format of "it's not {x}, it's {y}".
- Filler frames: "It's important to note," "It should be noted that," "In today's landscape," "In order to" (use "to").
- Empty intensifiers: very, truly, crucial, vital — unless quantified.
- Restating the question, re-summarizing what was just said, or repeating a point in new words.
- Hedging as padding (may/might/could). If uncertain, state what is uncertain and why.

### Final check

Delete any sentence whose removal loses nothing.
